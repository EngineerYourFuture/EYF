## 4. Real Problems EYF Solves

### The 3 Root Causes of Placement Failure

#### Root Cause 1: Wrong Inputs
Students practice random problems instead of company-pattern-targeted prep. Nobody maps practice to actual OA fingerprints.

**EYF Solution:** Company OA Fingerprint Engine — exact distribution of question types, difficulty, and patterns tested per company in the last 2 years. "You've practiced 40% of what Amazon actually tests. Here are your 3 gaps."

#### Root Cause 2: Wrong Measurement
Students measure effort (problems solved) not readiness (can I solve this under 30-minute pressure?). Every competitor rewards the wrong metric.

**EYF Solution:** Placement Readiness Score replaces problem counter — composite of pattern coverage, timed accuracy, mock performance, cognitive score, peer benchmark.

#### Root Cause 3: No Feedback Loop
Students fail, don't know why, repeat same prep, fail again.

**EYF Solution:** Rejection Debrief System — post-rejection AI cross-references practice history to identify the exact failure mode and prescribe targeted improvement.

### Critical Gaps Nobody Is Solving

#### The Pressure Gap
Students solve problems at home but freeze in interviews. Nobody trains specifically for performance under observation.

**Solution:** Progressive Pressure Inoculation Mode
- Week 1: Normal timed practice
- Week 2: AI observer prompts every 5 min ("you haven't typed in 3 minutes")
- Week 3: Random "explain your approach" interruptions
- Week 4: Full simulation with freeze detection (90s no typing → Socratic nudge)

#### The Memorisation vs Pattern Gap
Students memorise solutions. Companies give variants. Students fail.

**Solution:** Auto-generated variant system — after every solved problem, surface 2-3 variants testing the same pattern with a twist. Forces pattern generalisation.

#### The OA Dark Room
80% of students fail at OA before any human sees their resume. No platform simulates the actual OA interface (HackerRank, Mettl, HirePro).

**Solution:** OA Interface Simulator — exact clone of HackerRank, HackerEarth, Mettl interfaces. Students who've seen the environment before perform 20%+ better.

#### The Honest Calibration Gap
"I want FAANG" but 92% aren't on track. Nobody gives honest data-driven redirect.

**Solution:** Placement Reality Calibrator
- "Realistic now (6 months): Freshworks, Juspay, good startups"
- "Stretch with work (8 months): Flipkart, Meesho"
- "Dream (18+ months): Amazon, Google"

#### The Imposter Syndrome Gap
Tier 2/3 students believe they can't compete with IITians — even when they have the skills.

**Solution:** Proof Layer — "47% of Swiggy's last 50 EYF placements were from Tier 2/3 colleges. Here are 3 students from colleges like yours."

#### The Post-Rejection Spiral
Students get rejected, have no data, repeat same prep, get rejected again.

**Solution:** Rejection Debrief — structured 5-field form post-rejection. AI hypothesis: "Your avg graph solve time is 42 min but OA gives 30 min. This likely caused your failure."

#### The Accountability Collapse
Day 1-7: motivated. Day 14: inconsistent. Day 30: churned.

**Solution:** Smart Accountability Pods — groups of 4 matched on target company tier, timeline, skill level, availability. Daily 2-min async check-in + weekly 30-min video peer session.

#### HR Round Blindspot
40-60% of students who clear technical are eliminated in HR. Zero platforms prep for this seriously.

**Solution:** HR Round Mastery — AI behavioral mock with STAR method training, company-specific HR question banks, communication score tracking.

#### Off-Campus Drive Blackhole
Most good jobs for Tier 2/3 are off-campus but discovery is chaotic.

**Solution:** Off-Campus Drive Radar — automated aggregation with eligibility matching (CGPA cutoff, graduation year, branch) and personal alerts.

## 5A. Career Tracks — Choose Your Path

### What This Feature Is

> **Pick your engineering role. Get a week-by-week curriculum, company targets, and direct links to every resource you need on EYF.**

Most prep platforms treat every student the same — one DSA sheet, one roadmap, one path. The reality: a Frontend Engineer and a DevOps Engineer need completely different skills, different interview formats, different company targets, and different portfolios. EYF's Career Tracks solves this by giving every student a role-specific personalised journey from Day 1.

### Why This Is a Major Differentiator

| What competitors do | What EYF does |
|---|---|
| One generic DSA roadmap for all | 12+ role-specific week-by-week curricula |
| Same problems for frontend and backend | Pattern weighting adjusted per role |
| No role-specific company targets | Companies filtered by what they hire for this role |
| No tech stack guidance | Tech stack + projects tailored to the role |
| No portfolio guidance | Portfolio project list specific to the role and companies |

### How Career Tracks Work

1. **User selects their target role** during onboarding or from the "Choose Your Path" dashboard
2. **Assessment adapts** — skill assessment asks role-relevant questions (Frontend student gets React/CSS questions, not Linux kernel questions)
3. **Roadmap is generated** — week-by-week curriculum built for that role, mixing DSA, tech skills, core subjects, and projects in the right ratio
4. **Company targets populated** — companies that actively hire for that role, with their specific interview format
5. **Resources linked** — every week's task links directly to EYF problems, theory notes, mock types, and project guides
6. **User can switch tracks** — if a Frontend student decides to go Full Stack at Week 6, EYF recalculates and adjusts, preserving completed progress

### Track 1: Frontend Engineer

**Role summary:** Builds user interfaces. Companies test CSS/JS fundamentals, React depth, performance, and surprisingly — DSA at medium difficulty.

#### Skills Curriculum (16 weeks)

| Phase | Weeks | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation | 1–3 | HTML semantics, CSS (Flexbox, Grid, animations), JavaScript (ES6+, async/await, closures, event loop) |
| DSA for Frontend | 4–6 | Arrays, Strings, Trees (DOM is a tree), Graphs (component dependency), Easy-Medium LeetCode. Heavy DSA NOT needed. |
| React & Ecosystem | 7–9 | React core (hooks, context, rendering), React Router, State management (Redux/Zustand), Testing (Jest, RTL) |
| Performance & Web | 10–12 | Browser rendering pipeline, Critical rendering path, Web Vitals (LCP/CLS/INP), lazy loading, code splitting, bundle optimisation |
| Advanced & System | 13–14 | Micro-frontends, SSR/SSG/ISR (Next.js), PWA, WebSockets, Web Workers |
| Interview Prep | 15–16 | Machine coding rounds, UI component building under time pressure, system design (design Twitter feed) |

#### DSA Focus for Frontend Roles
- **Heavy:** Arrays, Strings, Hash Maps, Trees (DOM traversal questions)
- **Medium:** Graphs, Recursion, Sorting
- **Light:** DP, Advanced graphs — rarely asked for pure Frontend roles
- **Special:** Browser-specific DSA questions (event delegation, virtual DOM diffing algorithms)

#### Core Subjects Focus
- **Must know:** JavaScript engine internals (V8), Browser storage (cookies/localStorage/sessionStorage), HTTP/HTTPS, CORS, CSP
- **Good to know:** Basic OS (processes for Web Workers), Basic CN (how DNS works, CDN)
- **Skip for Frontend:** Deep DBMS, OS scheduling, CN protocols

#### Machine Coding Rounds (Unique to Frontend)
- Build a todo app with full CRUD in 45 minutes
- Implement infinite scroll with API integration
- Build a real-time search with debouncing
- Create a drag-and-drop Kanban board
- Implement a date picker component from scratch

#### Company Targets

| Tier | Companies | Interview Style |
|---|---|---|
| FAANG | Google, Meta, Amazon (frontend roles) | DSA heavy + system design + machine coding |
| Product Unicorn | Swiggy, Razorpay, CRED, Zepto, Groww | Machine coding + DSA medium + React depth |
| Mid-size Product | Atlassian, Freshworks, BrowserStack, Postman | Component building + performance + React |
| Startup | Any funded startup with React/Next.js stack | Portfolio + machine coding + culture fit |

#### Portfolio Projects for Frontend
- Design system + component library (shows React + CSS mastery)
- Real-time collaborative document editor (shows WebSockets + state management)
- E-commerce frontend with performance score > 90 (shows web vitals mastery)
- Chrome extension (shows browser APIs knowledge)

## 10. Low-Level Design (LLD)

### Service Architecture Patterns
- **Domain-driven design** — each microservice owns one domain, no cross-service DB queries
- **Event-driven communication** — services communicate via events (Redis Streams), no synchronous chaining
- **API-first contracts** — OpenAPI specs defined before implementation
- **CQRS for high-read services** — separate read/write models for problem/assessment service

### Critical Data Flows

#### User Solves a Problem
```
User submits code → API Gateway → DSA Service
→ DSA Service sends to Judge0 (Docker isolated)
→ Judge returns verdict → DSA Service processes result
→ Emits event: problem_solved → Analytics Service, Roadmap Service
→ Roadmap Service updates progress → unlocks next problem
→ Analytics Service updates streak, XP, leaderboard
→ Spaced repetition engine schedules review
→ Code DNA system updates coding pattern analysis
```

#### Skill Assessment Flow
```
Assessment Service generates adaptive question set based on target role
→ User answers timed questions (frontend timer, server validates)
→ Placement probability engine computes company-specific scores
→ Gap analysis generated per topic and company
→ Roadmap Service generates personalised study plan
→ Company calibration shown: realistic / stretch / dream targets
```

#### Mock Interview Flow
```
User books mock → Interview Service creates session
AI mock: Claude API receives transcript chunks → real-time feedback
Voice: Browser mic → Whisper API transcription → Claude processes
Post-interview: AI analyses full transcript → structured report
Report stored → feeds progress dashboard + anxiety index update
```

### Code Judge Architecture
- **Judge0 self-hosted** on isolated Docker containers
- **Never execute** user code on main application servers
- **Resource limits:** CPU time (1-2s), memory (256MB), network disabled
- **Additional sandboxing:** gVisor or nsjail for kernel-level isolation
- **Queue submissions:** BullMQ absorbs traffic spikes, workers scale horizontally
- **Result caching:** Cache identical submissions to reduce judge load

### Cognitive Games Engine LLD
- **State machine:** CREATED → IN_PROGRESS → PAUSED → SUBMITTED → EVALUATED
- **Anti-cheat:** Tab switch detection, copy-paste disable, randomised question order
- **Adaptive algorithm:** ELO-like rating adjusts difficulty per question type
- **Company simulation:** Each company's test format stored as a JSON configuration

## 14. DSA Learning Engine

### Problem Classification
- **15 Core Patterns:** Two Pointers, Sliding Window, Fast & Slow Pointers, Merge Intervals, Cyclic Sort, In-place Linked List Reversal, BFS, DFS, Two Heaps, Subsets, Binary Search, Top K Elements, K-way Merge, DP, Topological Sort
- **Difficulty tiers:** Beginner, Easy, Medium, Hard, Expert — mapped to interview frequency
- **Company tagging:** Every problem tagged to companies that asked it in the last 2 years

### Variant Generator System
After every solved problem, automatically surface 2-3 variants:
- "You solved Two Sum → Two Sum II (sorted array) → Two Sum on BST → Pair with Target Difference"
- AI generates variants from template library using Claude API
- Forces pattern generalisation, not memorisation

### Spaced Repetition System
- Problems rated by user performance (independent of global difficulty)
- SM-2 algorithm resurfaces problems at optimal intervals
- "Revisit" queue shown in daily dashboard
- Skill decay alerts: "You haven't practiced graphs in 23 days — skill decay begins at 21 days"

### Code Editor
- **Monaco Editor** (VS Code engine) with syntax highlighting, autocomplete
- **Languages:** C++17, Java 17, Python 3.11, JavaScript (Node 18)
- Run against custom test cases before hidden test cases
- Submission history with diff view
- Pressure mode: progressive stress inoculation with AI observer

### Algorithm Visualizer

#### 2D Visualizer (Build First — Phase 1)
- **Algorithms covered:** Arrays (two pointers, sliding window, prefix sum), Linked Lists (reversal, merge, cycle detection), Stacks & Queues, Binary Trees (inorder/preorder/postorder/level-order), BST (insert/delete/search), Graphs (BFS, DFS, shortest path, topological sort), Sorting (bubble, merge, quick, heap), DP table filling (knapsack, LCS, coin change), Tries, Heaps
- **Tech:** D3.js + React — renders fully in browser, zero server dependency
- **Controls:** Speed control (0.25× to 4×), pause/resume, step forward, step backward one frame at a time
- **Custom input:** User types their own array/tree/graph values → watches it animate on their data, not a fixed demo
- **Problem-linked:** Every problem page has a "Visualize this algorithm" button that opens the exact visualizer for that pattern
- **Mobile-optimised:** Smooth on budget Android phones (Redmi, Realme) — lightweight SVG-based rendering, no WebGL
- **Highlight mode:** Currently executing line in the code editor highlights simultaneously with the animation step

#### 3D Visualizer (Phase 2 — Differentiator & Marketing)
- **Tech:** Three.js / WebGL — 3D animated data structures
- **What it shows:** Sorting as rising/falling 3D bars, binary tree as floating nodes in space, graph BFS as ripple waves, stack push/pop as 3D blocks
- **Interaction:** Rotate, zoom, pan during animation. Pause and inspect any node
- **Shareable GIF:** Record a 10-second GIF of any visualization → one-click share to Instagram/Twitter
- **Device requirement:** Desktop or high-end device only — clearly labeled, not shown on low-end mobile
- **Purpose:** Learning aid + social virality. "This is how quicksort works" posts drive organic acquisition

## 32. Security & Compliance

### Code Execution Security (Highest Risk)
> ⚠️ Never execute user code on main application servers

- **Judge0 isolation:** Docker with CPU time limits, memory limits, no network, no filesystem writes
- **gVisor/nsjail:** Additional kernel-level sandboxing for container escape prevention
- **Input sanitisation:** Strip dangerous imports and system calls before judge
- **Rate limiting:** Max 5 submissions/minute per user

### Platform Security
- **SQL injection:** Prisma ORM or parameterised queries exclusively
- **XSS:** Sanitise all user-generated content (comments, experience submissions)
- **CORS:** Whitelist only `eyf.in`, `app.eyf.in`, `api.eyf.in`
- **WAF:** Cloudflare WAF — block OWASP Top 10 at edge
- **Auth brute force:** 5 attempts/hour limit on login endpoints

### Data Privacy
- **PII minimisation:** Only collect email/phone, name, college, graduation year, target role
- **Interview recordings:** Encrypted in R2/S3. Only accessible to session participants. Auto-delete after 90 days
- **Payment data:** Razorpay handles all card/UPI data. EYF never stores payment credentials
- **Passwords:** Handled entirely by Clerk/Supabase Auth

### India Compliance
- **IT Act 2000 + DPDP Act 2023** — Privacy policy and consent flows required
- **Terms of Service** — explicit clause on code submission ownership, community content, mentor liability
- **Data deletion** — implement GDPR-style deletion even without EU users (shows maturity)

## 40. Onboarding & Retention

### Onboarding Flow (First 10 Minutes)
1. **Signup:** Google OAuth or Phone OTP — 2 fields max
2. **Profile:** College, graduation year, target role, hours/day (60 seconds)
3. **Skill assessment:** 20 minutes — framed as "Let's find your starting point"
4. **Gap report + calibration:** Realistic / stretch / dream companies shown
5. **Roadmap generated:** "Day 1: Two Pointers — 3 problems + 1 concept"
6. **First problem:** Solve it now. Never leave without a win.

### First Week Retention
- Day 1 email: "Your roadmap is ready — here's your first challenge"
- Day 3 push: "You've solved 6 problems! You're in the top 30% of users at your stage"
- Day 7 milestone: "7-day streak! You've unlocked the Mock Interview module"

### 30-Day Churn Prevention
- Health score monitoring: 3 days no login → re-engagement push
- Progress milestone emails: celebrate topic completion + next steps
- Peer comparison: "Students at your college who started the same week have solved 45 problems. You're at 28."
- Feature discovery: introduce one new feature per week via in-app tooltip
- Mood check-in: 3 consecutive low days → "comeback stories" + lighter plan
- Accountability pod nudge: pod members notified if you go silent

### Mental Health Layer
- Daily mood check-in (emoji scale, 5 seconds)
- Low days trigger: real placement stats ("73% of placed students had 2+ rejection streaks")
- Curated "comeback stories" feed for struggling students
- Optional 30-min peer check-in from accountability pod
- No toxic hustle culture — sustainable prep messaging throughout
